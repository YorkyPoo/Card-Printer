Phonics Flash Cards

Here are your phonics cards. To print them as 2.5" x 3.5" cards, just use your browser's Print function (Ctrl+P or Cmd+P). The page is specially styled to format them correctly on letter-sized paper.

Closed Syllable

🚪

A syllable with one vowel followed by one or more consonants. The vowel sound is short.

Examples:
  • cat
  • pen
  • sit
  • hop
  • cup

Open Syllable

🚪

A syllable that ends in one vowel. The vowel sound is long (it says its name).

Examples:
  • hi
  • me
  • go
  • she
  • we

Magic 'e' (VCe)

✨

The Magic 'e' at the end is silent. It jumps over one consonant to make the first vowel long (say its name).

Examples:
  • cap ➔ cape
  • kit ➔ kite
  • hop ➔ hope
  • cub ➔ cube

FLOSS Rule

🦷

In a one-syllable word with a short vowel, double the f, l, s, and z at the end.

Examples:
  • stuff
  • bell
  • miss
  • buzz

C or K?

🐱 🪁

"K" takes i and e. (Kite Rule)
"C" takes a, o, and u. (Cat Rule)

  • K: key, kind, kiss
  • C: cat, cot, cup

Ending /k/ Sound

🦆 🎂

Use -ck after a short vowel.
Use -ke after a long vowel (Magic 'e').

  • Short: duck, sock, pack
  • Long: bake, like, bike

Digraphs

🤝

Two letters that stick together to make one new sound.

Examples:
  • ship
  • chip
  • thin
  • whisk

More Digraphs

🗣️

More letter teams that make one sound!

Examples:
  • phone (/f/)
  • laugh (/f/)
  • bridge (/j/)
  • duck (/k/)

Vowel Teams

👟👟

"When two vowels go walking, the first one does the talking" (it says its long name).

Examples:
  • boat (says o)
  • sail (says a)
  • green (says e)
  • pie (says i)

Diphthongs

🤕

Two vowels that make a special, sliding sound. (Like the "ow" in "ouch!")

Examples:
  • cloud
  • cow
  • coin
  • boy

Bossy 'r'

🏴‍☠️

The 'r' is the boss! It takes control and changes the vowel's sound.

Examples:
  • car
  • fork
  • her, bird, turn

Soft C & G

🧊

C and G say their soft sounds (/s/ and /j/) when followed by e, i, or y.

  • Soft C: cent, city, cycle
  • Soft G: gem, giant, gym

'Y' as a Vowel

🦋 🐇

1-Syllable: 'y' says /ī/ (long i) like fly.
2-Syllables: 'y' says /ē/ (long e) like bunny.

  • /ī/: my, try, shy
  • /ē/: puppy, funny, happy

Sounds of -ed

⏰

The past tense suffix -ed makes three different sounds:

  • /t/ (like in jumped)
  • /d/ (like in filled)
  • /ed/ (like in wanted)

Doubling Rule (1-1-1)

1️⃣ 1️⃣ 1️⃣

For a 1-syllable word with 1 short vowel and 1 ending consonant... Double the consonant before adding a vowel suffix (-ing, -ed, -er, -est).

  • hop ➔ hopping, hopped
  • big ➔ bigger, biggest
  • run ➔ runner, running

Plurals (-s / -es)

🐈 🐈

Add -s to make most words plural.
Add -es to words ending in s, x, z, sh, ch.

  • cat ➔ cats
  • bus ➔ buses
  • box ➔ boxes
  • wish ➔ wishes

Consonant Blends

🌪️

Two or three consonants that blend together. You can still hear each letter's sound!

  • s-blends: stop, spin, slip
  • l-blends: block, clap, flag
  • r-blends: brag, crab, trip

Drop the 'e' Rule

✂️

When a word ends in a Magic 'e', drop the 'e' before adding a vowel suffix (-ing, -ed, -er).

Examples:
  • bake + ing ➔ baking
  • hope + ed ➔ hoped
  • wave + ing ➔ waving
  • like + ed ➔ liked

Change 'y' to 'i'

🔄

When a word ends in a consonant + y, change the 'y' to 'i' before adding a suffix (unless the suffix is -ing).

  • happy ➔ happier, happiest
  • fly ➔ flies, flied
  • But: fly + ing ➔ flying

Silent Letter Teams

🤫

Two letters at the beginning or end of a word where one is silent!

  • knee, know (/n/)
  • write, wrap (/r/)
  • comb, lamb (/m/)
  • gnat, sign (/n/)

Doubling Examples

🏃‍♀️💨

Verbs that follow the 1-1-1 Rule:

  • scrub ➔ scrubbing, scrubbed
  • stop ➔ stopping, stopped
  • hop ➔ hopping, hopped
  • clap ➔ clapping, clapped

Doubling Examples

🏃‍♂️💨

More verbs that follow the 1-1-1 Rule:

  • grab ➔ grabbing, grabbed
  • shred ➔ shredding, shredded
  • pin ➔ pinning, pinned
  • skip ➔ skipping, skipped

Doubling Examples

🏆

More verbs that follow the 1-1-1 Rule:

  • run ➔ running, runner
  • win ➔ winning, winner
  • rub ➔ rubbing, rubber
  • drum ➔ drumming, drummer
  • sit ➔ sitting, sitter

Doubling Examples

📈

Adjectives that follow the 1-1-1 Rule:

  • big ➔ bigger, biggest
  • hot ➔ hotter, hottest
  • sad ➔ sadder, saddest
  • fat ➔ fatter, fattest
  • thin ➔ thinner, thinnest
